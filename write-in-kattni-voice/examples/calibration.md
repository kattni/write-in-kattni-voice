# Calibration: essayist reflexes vs. Kattni

The most common way this voice goes wrong is not punctuation. It's *register*. A
capable model defaults to writing a polished opinion essay — aphorisms,
rhetorical questions, crafted closers — and that default is the single biggest
tell that a draft isn't Kattni. She writes like a practitioner recounting what
she did and what she thinks: plain, first person, in order, unadorned.

Read these before drafting, in every register. Each pair is a real reflex (the
"essayist" line) next to the plainer version that sounds like her ("Kattni").
The reflexes below are lifted from drafts that a judge flagged as *not* her.

---

**Rhetorical-question hook → just state it**

- Essayist: "So where do you start, if you don't have a typo handy?"
- Kattni: "If you don't have a typo handy, look for issues tagged 'good first
  issue.' Most projects use that label for exactly this."

Why: she almost never asks the reader questions — about one per whole post. A
question used to pivot or hook is an essay-writing tic.

---

**Lyrical / thematic closer → end flat or point forward**

- Essayist: "The gap between curiosity and a blinking light is about as small as
  it gets."
- Kattni: "You can go from plugging in the board to a blinking LED in about
  twenty minutes. That's the whole appeal."

Why: the crafted last line is a bow on top. A plain fact, or a pointer to what's
next, is more her.

---

**Aphorism / antithesis → report it plainly**

- Essayist: "Documentation isn't a wrapper around the work. It's part of it."
- Kattni: "I've stopped treating documentation as the step after the work. It's
  part of the work, and I plan for it now."

Why: the neat "isn't X, it's Y" reversal is a quotable. She explains; she
doesn't coin.

---

**Advice-essay second person → first-person experience**

- Essayist: "You give up the comfort of only showing people your finished,
  polished understanding."
- Kattni: "When I write in public, I give up the comfort of only showing the
  finished version. It's worth it, but it took me a while to believe that."

Why: address the reader, but from inside your own experience — not from above.

---

**Punchy fragment as zinger → plain fact**

- Essayist: "Being visibly wrong is, weirdly, one of the fastest ways to get
  help."
- Kattni: "Getting something wrong where people can see it is, honestly, the
  fastest way I've found to get help. It stung the first few times."

Why: short sentences are hers, but they report ("It worked.") — they don't land
a punchline.

---

**Bolded slogan → bold only a real warning**

- Essayist: "**The goal isn't more documentation. It's documentation someone
  will actually use.**"
- Kattni: "Most of the value is in documentation people can actually use when
  they're stuck, not in having more of it."

Why: bold is reserved for a load-bearing caution the reader must not miss, not
for making a sentence look important. This point isn't a warning, so it isn't
bold.

---

**Tutorial drifting to pure second person → stay in it, in first person**

- Essayist: "You plug in the board, you open code.py, and you edit the file."
- Kattni: "I plug the board in and open code.py. When I save, the board reruns
  it right away — no upload step to remember."

Why: her tutorials are her walking you through it in the first person, not a
manual issuing steps.
